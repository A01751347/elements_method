After designing immersions for leaders and teams, the mistakes we see repeat with almost comic regularity. None of them has to do with budget. All of them have to do with the order in which decisions get made.

## Mistake 1. Choosing the venue before the objective

The most common one. Someone finds a beautiful place, the date gets booked, and then people think about what to do there. The venue should be the last decision, not the first: it serves the objective, not the other way around.

**How to avoid it:** before quoting anything, leadership and HR agree on a single sentence: "When we come back, ______ has to be different." With that sentence, the right venue becomes obvious.

## Mistake 2. Overfilling the agenda

Presentations from every department, quarterly results, the annual plan, a dynamic at the end if there's time left. It's the office with a better view. Nobody comes back clearer because nobody had room to think.

**How to avoid it:** fewer, deeper blocks. In our [offsites](/en/company-offsites-mexico), each day follows a six-phase arc (release, encounter, methodology, reflection, dialogue and integration) and strategic work comes after lowering the noise, never before.

## Mistake 3. Inviting too many people

"So nobody feels excluded", 40 people get invited to a retreat whose objective was to align the executive committee. With more than 20 people in a room, the work turns superficial: nobody says what they think.

**How to avoid it:** the objective defines the group. If more people need to be involved, work in cohorts or design a second moment. Our corporate programs start at 4 people and, when the group is large, it gets split.

## Mistake 4. Hiring activities instead of a method

Zipline, team cooking, a motivational talk. They produce anecdotes and photos. They don't change how decisions get made on Monday. The question almost nobody asks the provider is: which conversation does each activity open, and who facilitates it when it opens?

**How to avoid it:** ask for the method, the sequence and the facilitators' track record. At [Elements Method](/en/leadership-retreats) the method is built on four elements (Earth, Fire, Water and Air) and integrates applied neuroscience, NLP, executive coaching and internal-regulation practices. Every activity exists because of the element it activates and the conversation it provokes.

## Mistake 5. Not designing what comes after

The retreat ends, everyone comes back inspired, and two weeks later nothing has changed. Not because the retreat was bad, but because nobody designed the integration.

**How to avoid it:** before leaving, written agreements, one owner per agreement and review dates (30, 60 and 90 days). Consider follow-up sessions or an additional module. Integration is a stage of the program, not a thank-you email.

## The underlying mistake

All five share one cause: planning the retreat as an event rather than as an intervention. An event gets organized; an intervention gets designed, from a diagnosis and with an expected result.
