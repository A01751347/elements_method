It is one of the most difficult questions I have seen in successful leaders.

We usually know when something is failing, but it is much harder to recognize when something is still working… and is no longer helping us grow.

The role you once dreamed about. The organization where you built your career. The leadership style that got you promoted. The team structure that helped you scale. The way you make decisions. Even the professional identity that helped you become successful.

None of them necessarily needs to be "wrong." You may simply have outgrown them.

## The leadership paradox

The behaviors that created your current level of success may become the behaviors that limit your next one.

- The highly involved leader eventually needs to delegate.
- The expert who always had the answer needs to become the leader who asks better questions.
- The executive who built a reputation on being constantly available needs stronger boundaries.
- The leader who personally drove every result needs to build an organization capable of succeeding without them.

The challenge is that we become attached to what worked.

## Questions for this inflection point

- What am I continuing to do simply because it made me successful in the past?
- What does my current role require from me?
- Where have my strengths become limitations?
- What am I holding onto because it is familiar rather than because it is effective?
- What part of my leadership identity needs to evolve for my organization — and me — to reach the next level?
- If I were designing the leader I need to become for the next five years, what would I stop doing today?

## Growth doesn't always mean walking away

Growth doesn't always require leaving.

Sometimes it means evolving while you stay. Sometimes it means changing how you lead. Sometimes it means creating space for others to step up.

And yes, sometimes it means recognizing that a role, an organization, a partnership or a chapter that was once exactly right for you has completed its purpose.

> Before redesigning the strategy, it is worth redesigning the internal state you are deciding it from.
