It's the first question companies ask and the one fewest providers answer clearly. The honest answer is "it depends", but it depends on five concrete variables. Once you know them, you can compare proposals that at first sight look nothing alike.

## The five variables

### 1. Number of people

The variable that weighs the most and the least linear one. A group of 8 and a group of 15 don't cost twice as much as each other: facilitation scales by tiers, not per person. Past a certain size (around 20 people in a room) the work turns superficial and it's better to split into cohorts, which does add cost. Our corporate programs start at 4 people.

### 2. Duration: sessions or days

One intensive day, two or three days of immersion, or a modular program over several months. Every additional session or day adds facilitation and logistics, but multi-session programs usually get volume discounts, because diagnosis and design happen only once.

### 3. Modality

In person, virtual or hybrid. Virtual is the cheapest and the least powerful; hybrid combines short remote sessions with one in-person encounter. For deep leadership work, presence in nature is still what produces the change.

### 4. Venue and logistics

Lodging, meals, rooms and transfers. This is where the price varies most between proposals, and where value gets confused most often: a spectacular venue doesn't make up for weak facilitation. Mexico's advantage is nature less than two hours from the big cities: we work with venues in Morelos and Mexico City, or adapt the program to the location the team already has.

### 5. Facilitation and method

The invisible variable in most quotes and the one that decides whether the retreat works. Ask which method is used, who facilitates, how many facilitators per group and what happens after the retreat. A proposal without prior diagnosis or subsequent integration is quoting an event, not an intervention.

## How to compare proposals that look nothing alike

Put them all in one table with these columns: people, days, modality, what the venue includes, number of facilitators, prior diagnosis, integration afterwards, languages. What usually shows up is that the "expensive" proposal includes three things the "cheap" one never mentions.
