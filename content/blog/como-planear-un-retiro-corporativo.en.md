In most companies, a corporate retreat gets planned backwards: first the date, then the hotel, then "let's see what we do". The result is predictable. This guide follows the order that actually works.

## Step 1. Write the objective in one sentence

Before any quote, leadership and HR must agree on a single sentence: "When we come back, ______ has to be different." Real examples:

- "The executive committee has to agree on the three priorities of the year."
- "Sales and product have to stop working like two separate companies."
- "The new directors have to know each other before high season."

If it doesn't fit in one sentence, it isn't an objective yet: it's a wish list.

## Step 2. Decide who goes (and who doesn't)

The objective defines the group, not the org chart. A strategic alignment retreat with 40 people aligns nothing; an integration retreat without the department heads doesn't either. As a rule, more than 20 people in one room makes the work superficial: split into cohorts or reduce the group.

## Step 3. Choose the format

With a clear objective and group, the format becomes obvious:

- **One intensive day** for a narrow objective or a team that can't be away longer.
- **Two or three days** for deep leadership work or strategic alignment.
- **Modular program** (one module per month) for sustained development.
- **Hybrid or virtual** for distributed teams.

At [Elements Method](/en/corporate-retreats) every corporate program goes through three stages: team diagnosis, immersion in nature, and integration afterwards.

## Step 4. The venue is chosen last, not first

The venue serves the objective. For leadership work, nature isn't a luxury: lowering operational noise is what makes possible the conversation that gets postponed at the office. Mexico has a huge advantage: nature less than two hours from the big cities. We work with venues in Morelos (Misión del Sol, in Jiutepec) and spaces in Mexico City, or adapt the program to the location the team already has.

## Step 5. Facilitation: the variable that weighs the most

Ask any provider three things:

1. What method do you use and why that sequence of activities?
2. Who facilitates and what is their track record?
3. Can you work in Spanish and English with the same group?

In our case, every immersion follows the same six-phase arc (release, encounter, methodology, reflection, dialogue and integration) with tools from applied neuroscience, NLP, executive coaching and internal-regulation practices.

## Step 6. Design what happens afterwards

A retreat without integration is forgotten in two weeks. Before you leave, define:

- Which agreements are written down and who sustains them.
- When they get reviewed (30, 60 and 90 days work well).
- Whether there will be follow-up sessions or an additional module.

## Six-week timeline

| Week | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 6 | One-sentence objective. Discovery with leadership and HR. |
| 5 | Team diagnosis. Group and format defined. |
| 4 | Venue confirmed. Proposal and quote closed. |
| 3 | Agenda designed. Communication to participants. |
| 2 | Logistics: travel, meals, materials. |
| 1 | Final adjustments with the team leader. |
| 0 | Retreat. |
| +2 | Review of agreements. Integration plan under way. |
